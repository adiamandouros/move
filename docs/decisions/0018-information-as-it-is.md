# 18. The information is given as it is, and says so

Decided 2026-10-29 · In force

## Situation

Wayhop passes on what OASA and STASY publish, and adds data of its own:
where to ride for an exit, which platforms are step-free, ticket prices,
the facts on the Info page. Any of it can be wrong or late, and someone
who needs a lift, or is catching the last train, may rely on it.

## Decision

- The terms of use (`/terms`, in English and Greek, beside Privacy) say
  that the information comes with no promise that it is right, complete,
  current or available; where it comes from, Wayhop's own data included;
  to check at the station, with STASY or with OASA when it matters; that
  Wayhop is not affiliated with them; and that it is not liable, as far as
  the law allows.
- Wherever Wayhop says a platform can be reached without steps, it also
  says that lifts can stop working, with a link to STASY's lift page
  (`liftCaution` in [metro.js](../../public/js/core/metro.js)): on the
  station pages, and in the subway planner's notice about platforms with
  steps.
- Like the privacy page, the terms change before what they describe does,
  and show the date they last changed.

## What follows

- A new kind of information names its source on the About page, and, if
  it's Wayhop's own, falls under the terms' "collected by Wayhop".
- A new place that states step-free access shows `liftCaution` too.

## Look again when

- A Greek lawyer has read Privacy and the terms (wanted before the ads go
  live), or the operator has to be named on the site.
- Wayhop starts taking reports from riders (item 11), which the terms would
  then cover.
